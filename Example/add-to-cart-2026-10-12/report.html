Add to cart

B2B products marketplace · desktop web · 12 Oct 2026 · purchasing staff buying from many vendors in one place

1. Summary

Most captured marketplaces show a unit price before asking for an account. They then split into two products that look the same: a real cart (Amazon Business, DHgate) or an inquiry that is named like a cart (Made-in-China’s Inquiry Basket, IndiaMART’s Get Latest Price, Global Sources’ Inquire Now). The minimum order is usually printed beside the price and then ignored by the form. The clearest gap is a quantity control that states the minimum and refuses a smaller number, next to a single obvious action that is either Add to cart or Request quote.

What changed since 10 Oct 2026

The 10 Oct Paper report looked at distributor catalogs (McMaster-Carr, Uline, WebstaurantStore) plus Alibaba. Memory had no stored run, so this comparison is from that Paper file. This run looked at multi-vendor marketplaces instead, because the product context is purchasing staff buying from many vendors in one place.

Amazon Business was unscored on 10 Oct as a registration gate. This time the public supplies catalog opened a guest product page, a guest cart, and sign-in only at checkout. Faire is still a signup wall. Alibaba was an inquiry flow on 10 Oct and a CAPTCHA this time, so it is unscored. McMaster-Carr’s one-click, stay-on-page add was the previous best pattern and was not in this set. The closest minimum-order behavior now is DHgate snapping quantity 1 back to 40.

2. Patterns

Price on the product, before an account

5 of 5 captured — Amazon Business, IndiaMART, Made-in-China, DHgate, Global Sources

Purchasing staff can compare vendors only if the number is on the product. Masking it until signup removes the decision this flow is for.

Inquiry standing in for the cart

3 of 5 captured — Made-in-China, IndiaMART, Global Sources

A fixed price and a quote request are different jobs. These three use one path for both, so a listed price still ends in a message to the supplier.

Quantity price tiers on the product

3 of 5 captured — Made-in-China, DHgate, Global Sources

The tier is the wholesale price. Showing it on the product matches the principle that price is visible when someone considers buying.

Minimum order printed, then weakly enforced

3 of 5 captured — DHgate, Made-in-China, Global Sources

A label is not enforcement. DHgate is the only one that changes the number to meet the minimum. The others let a too-small quantity sit in the form.

Account wall before the price

2 not captured — Faire, Ankorstore. Alibaba never reached a product (CAPTCHA).

This is the opposite of guest browsing and guest add-to-cart. The wholesale number never appears, so the flow could not be scored.

4. Edge cases

DHgate — quantity snaps to the minimum. Typing 1 in a field whose minimum is 40 returned the value to 40, with “Min. Order: 40 Piece” still visible. That is the only captured control that would not let a below-minimum number stand. See step-03 in the DHgate appendix.
DHgate — shipping block does not explain a failed add. The page already said the bottle cannot ship to India. Add to cart left the side cart at “Your cart is empty” and Rs.0.00, with no second message. See the same step-03 shot.
Made-in-China — minimum is not part of the inquiry error. The product tier starts at 200 pieces. Submitting quantity 1 flagged content, email, and the verification code only. See step-04.
Global Sources — inquiry quantity can sit under the printed minimum. The product says 2–999 pieces. The dialog opened at 2 and then kept 1 with no inline warning. See step-03.
IndiaMART — a shown price still ends in a phone login. ₹330/Piece is on the product. Get Latest Price asks for a mobile number and OTP before the buyer can send anything. See step-03. Checkout login is an intentional constraint; this wall is earlier than checkout.
Amazon Business — login is at checkout, not at the cart. A guest cart confirmed “Added to cart” and showed Proceed to checkout. That button then opened Sign in. See steps 03 and 04. That matches the constraint that an account is for checkout.

5. Opportunities

One control that states the minimum and holds it. Put “Minimum 40” inside the quantity field, not only in a caption. If someone types less, keep the number from proceeding and say what the floor is. DHgate already snaps the number back. Made-in-China and Global Sources print the floor and then let a smaller number through. This fits a decluttered page: one field, one rule.

Add to cart and Request quote as two different buttons, used one at a time. Use Add to cart only when the price is fixed. Use Request quote only when it is not. Amazon Business and DHgate only have the cart. IndiaMART, Made-in-China, and Global Sources only have the inquiry, even when a number is already on the page. IndiaMART’s “Get Latest Price” on a product that already says ₹330/Piece works against price being visible at the moment of the decision.

Keep the guest cart, and keep the account at checkout. Amazon Business already does this: browse, price, and cart without an account, then sign-in to check out. That is the stated constraint, so it is not a gap. Faire, Ankorstore, and IndiaMART put the account earlier, before the buyer can act on a price. Do not treat their walls as a pattern to copy.

Show the tier on the card, not only after the click. Global Sources and Made-in-China put a range and a minimum on the search card. Amazon Business shows one price and no minimum. For purchasing staff comparing vendors, the card should carry the unit price and the minimum so the product page is confirmation, not the first time the rule appears.
